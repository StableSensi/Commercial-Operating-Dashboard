Sales-to-Onboarding Process

A controlled process from qualified commercial interest to onboarding start.

Aryze commercial flow · Sales to onboarding · 2026

The Sales-to-Onboarding Process begins when an opportunity has been qualified and the customer needs a controlled path into onboarding. Its purpose is to make sure Sales, Legal, Compliance, Technical and Onboarding teams share the same context before work starts.

What changes

Handoff becomes readiness-based.

Sales does not pass a customer forward until product path, context, owner, blockers and next milestone are clear.

Why it matters

Onboarding starts with context.

The receiving team should not have to rediscover the deal, the use case or the customer's operating constraints.

What it enables

A cleaner implementation start.

Commercial fit, KYB, IT, legal, owners, blockers and next milestones become visible before the handoff.

Why the handoff needs a standard

The risk is that signed or high-intent customers arrive in onboarding without enough context, ownership or readiness to move efficiently.

A simple operating map for moving qualified opportunities into onboarding readiness while leaving room for focused follow-up where the customer needs more clarity.

Readiness review is not a repeat of sales discovery.

The process should preserve commercial context and add the legal, compliance and technical clarity needed for implementation.

Commercial readiness review

Purpose: Confirm that Sales has enough context for a controlled handoff.

  • Confirm the product path
  • Document use case and scope
  • Identify decision owner and stakeholders
  • Capture commercial commitments
  • Confirm urgency and next milestone
  • Decide whether KYB should start
Output: Fit, owner, next step and readiness gaps are clear.

Implementation readiness review

Purpose: Move from commercial commitment to onboarding clarity.

  • Bring IT, legal, compliance and operations into the same room
  • Review integration requirements
  • Review KYB status
  • Clarify legal and commercial blockers
  • Confirm responsibilities
  • Prepare onboarding handoff
Output: Requirements, blockers, owners and implementation path are clear.

If more alignment is needed, a follow-up meeting is added without breaking the process.

Onboarding Readiness Rules

These rules define what must be clear before a qualified opportunity becomes an onboarding case, when KYB starts, and when IT or legal should be involved.

01
Commercial context

Qualified opportunity

The customer has a real problem Aryze can solve, a relevant product path, enough intent to continue, and a realistic route toward implementation.

Operating ruleOnboarding work does not start from general interest. Sales must confirm product fit and customer intent first.
02
Known scope

Handoff packet

The opportunity has enough documented context for another team to understand the customer without restarting discovery.

  • Product path confirmed
  • Use case documented
  • Decision owner identified
  • Next milestone agreed
Operating ruleIf the receiving team cannot understand the case from the handoff packet, the handoff is not ready.
03
Readiness check

Implementation path

The team understands what must happen before onboarding can begin without avoidable friction.

  • Commercial scope
  • KYB status
  • Technical requirements
  • Legal blockers
  • Operational owner
Operating ruleUnknown blockers should be made visible before onboarding starts, not discovered after handoff.
04
Trust layer starts

KYB-ready

Product fit, customer intent, and the need to validate the company are clear enough before moving deeper into onboarding or implementation planning.

KYB triggerKYB starts when fit and intent are clear enough that continuing without compliance validation would create risk or delay.
05
Operational questions begin

Technical and legal review

The customer has moved beyond commercial interest and needs implementation, contract, integration, compliance, or operational questions answered.

  • Integration or API questions
  • Contract or responsibility questions
  • KYB document questions
  • Operational setup requirements
Operating ruleIT and legal join when the opportunity becomes operational, not when the deal is already stuck.
06
Structured handoff

Onboarding-ready

The product path, use case, owners, KYB status, technical requirements, legal blockers, and next milestone are clear enough for onboarding to begin.

Operating ruleOnboarding should not start from zero. Sales must provide context, decisions, blockers, owners, and next steps.

Sales owns the handoff packet. Onboarding should receive context, decisions, blockers, owners and next steps before work starts.

Follow-up meetings are part of the process, not a failure of the process.

Some opportunities need extra clarification before onboarding. The model allows for this without losing momentum.

Technical follow-up

Used when integration details, API questions, reporting, or operational flows need more review.

Legal follow-up

Used when contract terms, licensing assumptions, KYB documents, or responsibilities need more clarity.

Commercial follow-up

Used when pricing, scope, internal approvals, or decision process need more alignment.

Stakeholder follow-up

Used when additional customer stakeholders need to understand the product, risk, or onboarding path.
