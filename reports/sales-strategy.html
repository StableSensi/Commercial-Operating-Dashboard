Sales Strategy

How Aryze turns market activity into qualified opportunities and credible commercial commitments.

Aryze commercial strategy · 2026

Three products. Three buyers. Three motions. The strategy below defines who we sell to, how we qualify, where leads come from, and how the team should run the pipeline. The page ends where the Sales-to-Onboarding Process begins, at commercial commitment.

Focus

Right to win.

Concentrate on accounts where payment economics, deployment speed, licensing position or operating-model depth create a real reason to engage now.

Method

One motion per product.

Pay by Bank, Stablecoin Factory and Digital Cash each need a different conversation, cycle and proof point. Mixing them is the most common way deals are lost.

Discipline

Visible next steps.

Product path, decision owner and next milestone must be documented before management treats an opportunity as real.

Fit before volume.

Demand can come from many channels. Every signal passes the same standard: product fit, buyer intent, commercial value, and a clear next decision.

Avoid

Broad selling, loose ownership.

Generic pitchUnqualified interestLoose follow-upLate risk review

Activity, but no reliable view of fit or commercial commitment.

Three products, three motions.

Each product has its own ICP, buyer, cycle, proof point and priority market. Treat the cards below as the brief before every commercial conversation.

Pay by Bank

UK and EU merchants with payment-driven pain.

iGaming, betting, trading and card-under-served verticals with 100k+ tx/month and 35+ EUR average ticket, where card cost or chargeback exposure hits margin.

  • Geography: UK live (Volt). EU/EEA late Q2 2026 via Payabl and Volt.
  • Priority markets: UK gaming, EU iGaming, trading, VAMP-exposed merchants.
  • Anti-ICP: Outside UK+EU. Needs payouts or AIS.
  • Motion: Commercial-led. Demo plus savings calculator on the customer's own numbers.
  • Cycle: 6–12 weeks SMB. 3–6 months enterprise.
Real exampleTesco Bank uses Pay by Bank for credit-card payments. ~40% volume share. ~25% rolling 12-month growth (Mastercard case).
Stablecoin Factory

Builders and innovation teams with a concrete use case.

Founders, CTOs, product leads and innovation teams who want to deploy a simple stablecoin through a self-serve flow without enterprise complexity on day one.

  • Stage: Seed–Series A founders. Internal venture for enterprises.
  • Priority markets: Builders with validated use case. Innovation teams seeking controlled pilots.
  • Anti-ICP: Needs full institutional operation. Route to Digital Cash.
  • Motion: Product-led. Sandbox and docs before the first call.
  • Cycle: Weeks to a quarter (Own Programme). One to two quarters (Partner-Led). Two quarters+ (White-Label).
Real exampleNo named Factory customers in canonical source. The deployment experience is the proof point until the first reference closes.
Digital Cash

Already-licensed institutions ready to operate.

Banks, licensed financial institutions, fintech clusters and licensed conglomerates with a concrete settlement, treasury or liquidity use case.

  • Licence: Must already cover digital cash or related activity.
  • Priority markets: Tier-2/3 banks following JPM/Citi/BBVA/Santander. EMI/PI fintech clusters.
  • Anti-ICP: Unlicensed buyer. No exec sponsor identified.
  • Motion: Enterprise-led. The proof point is a multi-stakeholder workshop, not a demo.
  • Cycle: 6–18 months. Phase-1 possible in 4–6 months on narrow scope.
Real exampleNo named Digital Cash customers in canonical source. Peer references for context only: JPM Coin, Citi Token Services, BBVA, Santander.

An account that does not match an ICP can still be worth a conversation. Treat it as relationship work, not pipeline.

One persona per product.

The decision sits with one person. Selling to anyone else extends the cycle.

Pay by Bank

Payments Operations Lead

Or Head of Payments, CFO, COO. Lives in reconciliation cycles, chargeback rates, VAMP exposure and integration drift.

Needs to see: own numbers in the savings calculator. Honest scope on AIS and payouts.

Kills the deal: promising AIS, standalone payouts or recurring. Unsourced conversion-lift claims.

Stablecoin Factory

Founder, CTO or Head of Product

Shifts by motion. Own Programme: founder or CTO. Partner-Led: BD or distribution lead. White-Label: platform or product lead.

Needs to see: sandbox and docs without gating. Motion fit confirmed before commercials.

Kills the deal: selling Factory as lightweight Digital Cash. Promising RWA without product verification.

Digital Cash

Executive Sponsor

CFO, COO, Chief Digital Officer. Board-adjacent. Line leadership: Treasury, Transaction Banking, Payments, Digital Assets. Decisive but not daily: CIO/CTO, CCO/MLRO, General Counsel, Risk.

Needs to see: credible operating model. Architecture diagram. Legal pre-read. Multi-stakeholder workshop.

Kills the deal: "live in 6 weeks". "Plug-and-play". Positioning Aryze as issuer or operator.

What "qualified" means.

Six checks. Same for every product. Without them, the lead is interest, not pipeline.

1. Use case

One sentence the customer can repeat.

"Exploring" is not a use case.

2. Decision owner

Named, by title.

The right one for the product. Digital Cash does not close without an executive sponsor.

3. Budget framing

How the money gets found.

Card P&L impact (PbB). Motion-fit pricing (Factory). Multi-year capex/opex (Digital Cash).

4. Trigger event

Why this quarter, not next year.

VAMP threshold. Funding round. Compliance deadline. Peer move.

5. Licence position

Customer holds the licence.

Aryze is infrastructure, never issuer or operator. Factory and Digital Cash depend on this. PbB merchants operate under their own licence.

6. Technical capacity

An owner who can take it forward.

Payment owner + dev (PbB). Technical team for sandbox (Factory). Treasury+legal+compliance+tech engaged together (Digital Cash).

Sales process: signal to commitment.

Six stages from first signal to commercial commitment. Where the strategy ends, the Sales-to-Onboarding Process takes over.

Six stages. Two decision points.

Identify, qualify, prove relevance, align stakeholders, advance only when ownership and next decision are clear.

Top objections.

The three we hear most often, per product. The team should answer all three without rehearsing.

Pay by Bank

Three to expect.

  • "We already have an open-banking provider." Acknowledge. Pivot to the specific job their provider does not fill: theming, pricing, market depth.
  • "Our chargeback rate is fine." Ask the number. Reference VAMP April 2026 (1.5% threshold, acquirers hold at 0.5–0.7%).
  • "What about payouts?" Direct. Not productised today. Aryze covers deposits, top-ups and customer funding.
Stablecoin Factory

Three to expect.

  • "Why Aryze and not Fireblocks, Circle or Brale?" Acknowledge. Pivot to self-serve speed plus path upward to Digital Cash.
  • "Can Aryze issue the stablecoin?" Direct no. Customer is issuer.
  • "We need settlement, treasury and ramps day one." Re-route to Digital Cash.
Digital Cash

Three to expect.

  • "Why not build internally?" Acknowledge. 12–24 months and 5+ FTEs reality. Frame Aryze as a partnership, not a replacement.
  • "How do we know this is regulator-friendly?" Frame the model. Institution remains issuer and operator. Offer MLRO and GC workshop.
  • "How fast can we be live?" Honest 6–18 months. Phase-1 in 4–6 months on narrow scope.
